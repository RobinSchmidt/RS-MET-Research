\section{Integral Transforms}
In this section we will have a closer look at one particular class of linear operators that are called \emph{integral transforms}. These are important because they occur in a lot of scientific and engineering applications. These operators are typically built as follows: They take the function $f(x)$ and pointwise multiply it by another function $g_p(x)$ that is a member of a family of functions that is parameterized by some parameter $p$ and then they integrate that product over some interval. Because the multiplier function $g$ had a free parameter $p$ in it, we'll get a result that will be a function of that parameter. So, our original function $f(x)$ gets transformed into a function $F(p)$. The general anatomy of an integral transform looks like:
\begin{equation}
F(p) = \int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, g_p(x) \; dx
\end{equation}
